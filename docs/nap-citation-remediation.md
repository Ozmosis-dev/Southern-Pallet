## Separation strategy for the Summerford conflict

### 1. Establish the new entity before suppressing the old one

Create and verify Southern Pallet Recycling at Poplarville using the canonical NAP, permanent exterior signage, a utility bill or lease, business registration/DBA documentation, and photos showing the business operating at the address. A live local phone answered as “Southern Pallet Recycling” is stronger than relying only on web forms.

### 2. Treat Summerford as a former occupant, not a duplicate

The businesses are distinct. Use “Moved,” “Doesn’t exist here,” or “No longer at this location” when a platform offers those reasons. Do not merge listings, rename the Summerford profile, or request review transfer. Those actions can combine entity histories and make the conflict harder to unwind.

### 3. Request a first-party cleanup from Summerford

Send one documented request asking Summerford to remove the Poplarville address and Mississippi phone from its website, location feeds, recruiting profiles, and directory accounts. Include the exact URLs above, the date the relationship ended, and the correct remaining Summerford location if known. A correction made at Summerford’s first-party website is likely to reduce recurring data-aggregator recontamination.

### 4. Correct upstream sources in a controlled order

Recommended sequence:

1. Google Business Profile and Google Maps
2. Apple Business Connect and Bing Places
3. Data Axle and Foursquare
4. Yellow Pages, Yelp, BBB, Waze, and Manta
5. Indeed and other employment profiles
6. Pallet-industry associations and local chambers

Use the same name, address formatting, phone, URL, hours, categories, and logo on every submission. Keep a spreadsheet with the listing URL, login owner, submission date, case number, evidence supplied, and next follow-up date.

### 5. Escalate with evidence, not competing edits

If a suggested edit is denied, submit the platform’s support or redressal form with a concise statement: Summerford Pallet is a former partner and no longer operates at 119 Industrial Park Dr; Southern Pallet Recycling is the current occupant. Attach signage, occupancy, phone, and registration evidence. Avoid repeatedly creating new profiles, which can trigger duplicate filters or suspensions.

### 6. Build independent entity signals

After the core profiles are accurate, obtain citations and links under the full name from the Poplarville/Pearl River County chamber, Mississippi business directories, pallet-industry associations, suppliers, customers, and recruiting sites. Publish facility photos and a concise location history on owned profiles. This gives search engines more corroboration than a single website/schema change.

## Google-specific guardrails

Google requires real-world names and accurate addresses, and businesses displaying an address should have permanent fixed signage. A satellite or coworking-style office is only eligible when it is signed, staffed by the business, and receives customers during business hours. See [Google’s representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en).

For a profile the company does not control, Google provides “Suggest an edit” and “Place is closed or not here,” including moved or nonexistent-location reasons. See [Google’s removal instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16043467?hl=en) and [Maps closure/move instructions](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/10552932?hl=en).
